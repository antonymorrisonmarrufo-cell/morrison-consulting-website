# Failure-Demand App — Gap Analysis vs Best-Practice Discovery

**A standalone, honest assessment of where the current process is weak when held against best-practice discovery/analysis for a large public-sector transformation** (GDS service manual, Seddon/Vanguard systems thinking, Lean Six Sigma DMAIC, HM Treasury Green Book Five Case Model).

This note deliberately sits *apart* from the process document. Its job is to be critical, not complete.

---

## Framing: transcripts as primary research

One received critique — "you rely on data mining, not primary user research" — needs qualifying, because it is only half right.

**The transcripts *are* primary research, at a scale and speed traditional discovery cannot match.** Fifty thousand real conversations give richer, less-biased evidence of failure, contact drivers and resident pain points than a handful of interviews or a workshop — and they arrive in days, not months. For a failure-demand programme this is a *strength*, not a shortcut.

The genuine residual gap is narrower than "no user research":

- **Silent demand** — residents who never made contact (gave up, or went to a councillor/MP). Partly recoverable from abandoned calls and failed web journeys, but never fully.
- **Unmet-need framing** — transcripts tell you why the *current* service failed; they are weaker at imagining a service that shouldn't exist in that form at all.

Both are mitigations to design in, not reasons to slow down. The rest of this note assumes transcripts remain the engine.

## Tier 1 — Material weaknesses

| # | Weakness | Best-practice reference | Why it matters at this scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Systemic causation is missing.** The model finds the root cause of a *call*, not the *system condition* that manufactured the demand. | Seddon / Vanguard "Check" | You risk treating symptoms and re-designing a broken flow rather than removing its cause. |
| 2 | **No end-to-end flow / value-stream view.** Contact is analysed per interaction, not as a journey across time and systems. | Value-stream mapping / DMAIC | Rework loops and repeat-contact chains — often the bulk of failure demand — stay invisible. |
| 3 | **Interventions are never tested with users before rollout.** The calibration pilot validates the *analysis*, not the *solution*. | GDS Alpha | You can ship a content/form redesign that still fails. |
| 4 | **Business case rigour is light.** No options appraisal, no Five Case Model, no dis-benefits, no cashable/non-cashable split. | Green Book Five Case Model | An FTE reduction (33 → 21 advisors) is only a *cashable* saving if posts are genuinely removed — the scrutiny report evidences this: £657k of the £914k recurring benefit is banked staffing budget reduction. |
| 5 | **No equalities / digital-inclusion lens.** DPIA present; EqIA and Public Sector Equality Duty analysis absent. | Equality Act 2010 s.149 | Pushing to digital while cutting headcount has real, legally-relevant differential impact. |
| 6 | **AI assurance stops at accuracy.** No bias/fairness, explainability, drift, transparency or data-ethics treatment. | UK Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard | Accurate ≠ fair or defensible for a public body processing residents' calls. |

## Tier 2 — Discipline gaps expected in a programme of this size

| Gap | Best-practice reference |
|---|---|
| Success measures are efficiency-only (volume, answer rate, FTE); no outcome measures or **counter-metrics/guardrails** to catch "gaming" (re-contact, first-contact resolution, satisfaction, exclusion). | Balanced scorecard |
| No RAID log, assumptions or dependency register. | MSP |
| Stakeholder mapping and comms/engagement plan under-developed (members, unions, front line). | MSP / change management |
| Baseline not benchmarked externally (LGA, CIPFA, peer councils, GovMetric). | Benchmarking |
| Prioritisation method undefined (no MoSCoW / WSJF / impact-effort scoring). | Agile prioritisation |
| No target operating model / BAU embedding or control plan — who owns and retrains the model in live. | DMAIC "Control" |
| No up-front data-quality assessment (transcript completeness, tagging reliability, CRM quality). | Data readiness |
| No programme plan — phasing, milestones, resourcing for the work itself. | Roadmap |

## Verdict

The process is **strong on analytical mechanics and genuinely good on data robustness**, but currently reads as a **data-analytics pipeline rather than a full discovery/transformation phase.** The missing pillars are the *systemic* and *human* ones: system-condition causation, end-to-end flow, test-with-users, the equalities and workforce lenses, and business-case rigour.

The encouraging part — addressed in the companion **Enhancement Roadmap** — is that most of these can be built as further enrichment passes on the transcript corpus already held, rather than as slow parallel workstreams.
